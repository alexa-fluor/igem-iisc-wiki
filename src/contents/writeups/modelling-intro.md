# Modelling and Computational Design

Before committing time and reagents to wet-lab validation, we used computational modelling to pre-screen as much of MOSKATEER's design as possible — testing feasibility, estimating costs to the bacterium, and refining components in silico before building them. Each experiment below addresses a distinct question the project needed answered.

Wet-lab work is expensive in ways that aren't always obvious until you're the one paying for it — reagents, time, and often a limited number of attempts before a deadline forces a decision one way or another. A fusion protein assembled from several distinct functional domains, expressed inside a bacterium that then has to survive being embedded in a sugar matrix and colonize a mosquito gut, has a lot of places where an untested design choice could quietly fail. Rather than discover those failure points at the bench, we treated modelling as a way to ask the cheap questions first: does this construct plausibly fold the way we intend, is the metabolic cost of expressing it something the bacterium can actually sustain, and are there design choices — a linker length, a domain order — that could be refined on a computer before they were ever cloned.

This mirrors the same lesson our mentors and PIs pushed throughout ideation: ask the answerable question first, and let its answer tell you whether the bigger question is even worth pursuing yet. Modelling gave that instinct a concrete home in the project — every wet-lab experiment described elsewhere on this wiki was, in some form, downstream of a modelling question we'd already asked and felt confident enough about to move forward on. That didn't mean modelling replaced the wet lab, or that every prediction held up once tested; it meant we went into the wet lab with fewer completely open questions, and a clearer sense of which uncertainties were worth spending reagents to resolve.

What follows is organized the same way our Experiments page is: each model or computational analysis is presented around the specific question it was built to answer, what we expected going in, and how it fed into the design decisions that followed.
