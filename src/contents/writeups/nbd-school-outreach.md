## School Outreach with NoteBook Drive

As part of our Human Practices efforts, MOSKATEER partnered with NoteBook Drive (NBD), a student-run initiative at IISc dedicated to supporting government schools in and around Bengaluru with resources and hands-on learning opportunities that these schools would otherwise struggle to access. Through this collaboration, we set out to bring malaria awareness directly to students in grades 1 through 8 — a group that rarely encounters synthetic biology or vector-borne disease education in their regular curriculum, but one whose households are often among the most directly affected by the disease.

At the center of this outreach were a set of activity sheets designed separately for younger and older students, so that each group engaged with material suited to their level. For the younger classes, this meant colouring pages built around the mosquito life cycle and picture-matching worksheets pairing simple icons — the *Anopheles* mosquito, its larva, a bed net, a patch of stagnant water — with their names, turning prevention concepts into something they could recognize and reinforce visually. For the older students, we introduced true-or-false fact sheets on malaria transmission and prevention, fill-in-the-blank exercises, and a matching activity linking DNA fragments to their corresponding amino acid sequences, giving grade 7–8 students an early, tangible introduction to the molecular biology underpinning the project itself, rather than just its public-health framing.

Beyond the worksheets, our members visited these schools in person to run the sessions directly, pairing the science content with creative, hands-on activities that kept younger students engaged well past a typical classroom talk. This included teaching origami and thumb painting alongside the malaria-focused material, giving the visits the feel of a fun classroom event rather than a lecture, while still leaving time for facilitator-led discussion, true/false card games, and male-versus-female mosquito flashcard demonstrations that tied back to how MOSKATEER's own sugar-trap system works. Each session closed with a small take-home element — a completed worksheet or a simple pledge card — so that what the students learned had a chance to reach their families as well.

This collaboration reflects a core part of our Human Practices philosophy: that a project addressing a disease as widespread and community-rooted as malaria has a responsibility to engage the communities it aims to serve, not only in adulthood or through clinical channels, but from a young age, in the classrooms where curiosity about science first takes shape. Partnering with NoteBook Drive let us reach government schools that our team would not otherwise have had a route into, and gave us a direct, grounded sense of what malaria awareness already looks like — and where it's missing — among the next generation growing up in the regions this project is ultimately meant to help.
